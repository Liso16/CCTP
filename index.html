About EaseAble

EaseAble is a dedicated healthcare platform designed to support individuals and families navigating neurological conditions, with a strong focus on autism. It offers an intelligent and accessible experience through advanced AI-powered analysis, allowing users to input observed behaviours and symptoms for guided insights based on established diagnostic criteria. The platform also features a structured screening section with targeted questions that support early understanding and informed decision-making. In addition, EaseAble provides a rich collection of educational resources tailored to empower families—helping them build awareness, develop practical skills, and enhance the overall quality of life for individuals with autism.

    2. -Does the child show a tendency in avoiding to make eye contact when being interacted with such as when being spoken to or during communication

  3. Are there delayed speech patterns ?

    4. -Whether the child display delayed speech development in comparison to others their age

  4. Does the child look at you when you call their name ?

    5. -Whether the child responds by looking at you when their name is called

  5. Does the child use simple gestures ?

    6. -(e.g. wave goodbye)

  7. Does the child follow where you're looking ?

  • Books and Articles on understanding autism
    • Understanding autism: insights from mind and brain

      This article explains autism as a complex neurodevelopmental condition mainly affecting social interaction, communication, and behaviour. It emphasises the role of the “social brain,” a network of brain regions responsible for understanding others’ emotions and intentions. Differences in this system help explain core autistic traits. The source highlights that autism is highly variable, with no single genetic cause, and diagnosis relies on behavioural observation. Advances in neuroscience, including brain imaging, improve understanding of early development and may enable earlier diagnosis and more personalised interventions, as brain systems remain adaptable over time

      Visit Article
    • Understanding autism

      This book presents autism as a layered, spectrum condition that varies widely between individuals. It explains that autism affects communication, behaviour, and how people perceive the world, but is not a disease—rather a difference in cognitive processing. The text highlights key features such as social communication differences, sensory sensitivities, and intense interests. It emphasises that no two autistic individuals are the same, challenging stereotypes. The book also explores evolving theories, including cognitive and neurological perspectives, and stresses the importance of supportive environments and personalised approaches to understanding and assisting autistic individuals.

      Visit Book
    • Understanding Autism Parents, Doctors, and the History of a Disorder

      This book explains autism as a condition whose meaning has evolved over time through interactions between medical professionals, researchers, and families. It highlights that autism is not only a clinical diagnosis but also shaped by social, cultural, and historical factors. A key argument is that parents have played a major role in influencing research, treatments, and understanding of autism through advocacy and lived experience. The text also explores how different theories—psychological, genetic, and biomedical—have shaped perceptions of autism. Overall, it emphasises that understanding autism requires both scientific knowledge and recognition of human experience

    • Coping strategies used by parents of children with autism

      This article examines how parents manage the stress of raising a child with autism. It found that most parents demonstrate strong adaptability despite significant emotional and practical challenges. The most common coping strategies include seeking social support from friends, family, and support groups, as well as using “reframing,” where parents positively reinterpret difficult situations. S ome parents also rely on passive coping methods, though these are less effective. The study highlights the importance of professional guidance and early diagnosis, emphasising that support systems and coping skills are crucial for improving family well-being and resilience.

The study emphasises that outcomes vary significantly across individuals, reinforcing the spectrum nature of autism. Findings suggest that both internal characteristics (such as cognitive or emotional processes) and external influences (such as parenting or environment) play a role in development.     • Coping over time: the parents of children with autism

      This article examines how parental coping strategies evolve over an extended period. It highlights that raising a child with autism involves ongoing stress, but parents often adapt and develop more effective coping mechanisms over time. Early stages are characterised by higher emotional strain, while later stages show increased acceptance and resilience. Social support, experience, and access to resources play key roles in improving coping outcomes. The study emphasises that coping is a dynamic process rather than a fixed response, and long-term adaptation significantly influences family well-being.

  • Managing routines and behavior
    • Autism Spectrum Disorders — Diagnosis and Management

      This article describes autism as a neurodevelopmental condition characterised by impaired social communication and repetitive behaviours. It explains that autism arises from a complex interaction of genetic, environmental, and neurological factors. Diagnosis involves a comprehensive, step-by-step assessment, including behavioural observation and application of DSM-5 criteria. The study highlights the importance of identifying severity levels, language ability, and co-existing conditions. Management focuses mainly on non-pharmacological interventions, particularly behavioural therapies delivered by a multidisciplinary team. Although there is no cure, early diagnosis and personalised intervention can significantly improve functioning and quality of life.

      Visit Journal
    • RoutineAid: Externalizing Key Design Elements to Support Daily Routines of Individuals with Autism

      This paper explores how digital tools can help autistic individuals manage daily routines. It identifies key challenges such as anxiety from disrupted routines, difficulty planning tasks, and limited support for maintaining balanced habits. The researchers developed a gamified mobile application, RoutineAid, which supports routine management across areas like physical activity, diet, mindfulness, and sleep. A two-month study showed that features such as micro-planning and interactive feedback improved engagement and routine consistency. The study highlights the importance of personalised, structured, and motivating digital interventions for supporting independence in individuals with autism.

      Read Article
    • Evidence-Based Practices to Reduce Challenging Behaviors of Young Children With Autism

      This article explores the use of positive behavioural strategies to support individuals with developmental or behavioural challenges, including autism. It emphasises Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports (PBIS) as an evidence-based framework designed to improve behaviour through proactive, structured, and supportive approaches. The study highlights that such interventions focus on teaching appropriate behaviours rather than punishing negative ones, leading to improved social outcomes and reduced behavioural issues. It also notes that successful implementation requires consistency, trained professionals, and collaboration across school, home, and community settings to ensure long-term effectiveness.

      Read Journal
